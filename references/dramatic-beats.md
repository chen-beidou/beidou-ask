# Dramatic beat engine

Design beats before shots. Each beat changes information, pressure, space, behavior, or relationship.

## Universal causal spine

```text
objective → pressure → resistance → first visible consequence
         → counteraction → deeper exposure/impact → irreversible new state
```

Internally answer:

1. What does the focal character want now?
2. What prevents it?
3. What must be different at the end of this Clip?

If nothing changes, revise the scene before adding coverage.

## Dialogue causality

```text
probe/pressure → listener contains reaction → leak or deflection
→ speaker notices or misreads it → strategy changes → relation rebalances
```

## Action causality

```text
initiation → defense/evasion/contact → force and displacement
→ recovery choice → tactical state changes
```

## 15 seconds

Hold one primary objective and one or two decisive changes. End on an action, decision, reveal, or relation change rather than an arbitrary cut.

## 30 seconds

If the 30-second scene naturally contains a midpoint reversal, seconds **12–18** are a useful planning window, not a mandatory beat. Place the reversal where the causal scene spine demands it. The second half should not merely repeat the same pressure pattern; change information, tactic, leverage, spatial relation, emotion, or decision when the story earns it.

## Coverage rule

A new camera angle must reveal at least one new fact:

- spatial relation
- action initiation
- contact/force
- hidden hand or prop behavior
- listener interpretation
- trigger-word failure
- changed power or decision

Do not replay the same action or line simply to obtain another angle.

## Shot economy

One or two **well-blocked** shots beat forty angles. Do not buy coverage you do not need. Before adding a cut, ask: would moving the actors, not the camera, tell it better? Kurosawa derives tension from a single designed frame without cutting at all. When a shot reveals nothing the last shot did not, delete it.

## One shot, one state change

<!-- RULE:CUT.STATE_CHANGE -->
A new shot or camera angle must advance at least **one meaningful state dimension**: information, emotion, tactical advantage, spatial relation, physical action, or decision. Important reversals and major dramatic cuts should preferably advance two or more dimensions. Do not force an emotional change into a purely tactical, spatial, action, or insert shot. If only the camera changes and no meaningful state changes, the cut is decoration.

## Rhythm

Sequence shots by **perceptual need**, not a mechanical alternation recipe. Repeated size or repeated duration is valid when it creates pressure, ritual, deadlock, or observational calm. Change size, duration, viewpoint, or camera character when the scene's information/emotion/action state benefits from it.

- Contrast can create pulse: a held shot beside a short cut, or a wide after a cluster of close views.
- Repetition can also create pulse when deliberate; do not vary merely for variety.
- Around a reversal, accelerate, decelerate, or hold according to the dramatic consequence rather than a fixed faster-cut rule.
- End on the state the next edit needs: a held destination image, a cut-on-action hand-off, a blackout, or an unresolved cliff edge are all valid when motivated.

## Cut on action

Place the cut inside a motion (a head turning, a hand pushing off, a door swinging) rather than after it. Cutting on the action hides the seam and keeps energy. Note the cut point in the shot line when it matters.
